Minimax: searching every future

This is the real algorithm from minmax.py, running live on a real position. O (pink) is to move with three empty cells. Watch it try each move, play out the replies, and pass values back up. Where two different move orders reach the same position, it reuses the cached answer.

Press Play.
+1 = X wins, 0 = draw, -1 = O wins. O minimises, X maximises. Dashed box = cache hit.

The neural net: one pass, no search

Same position. The board is flipped to the mover's point of view (cell * player), flows through 9 → 128 → 128 → 9, and out come nine scores. Bad cells are masked and the highest left is played.

The slider morphs the output from untrained noise to a trained shape. Illustrative: these are not weights from best.pt.
Hidden layers show 8 of 128 neurons each. Every neuron mixes all the previous layer's values, then ReLU keeps the positive ones.

Why a network at all?

Tic-tac-toe is small enough to enumerate, so minimax wins. Most interesting problems are not. Bars are powers of ten (log scale); the dashed line is roughly what a laptop can enumerate.

Tic-tac-toe
5,478 positions: search it all
Chess
about 1044 positions: impossible to enumerate
Go
about 10170 positions
100 words of text
100 tokens from a 100,000-token vocabulary = 10500 strings, across every language
The idea they share: stop enumerating, learn a function that scores options. One honest caveat for the Q&A: ChatGPT-style models are transformers trained to predict the next token, not a DQN. Your network is a small, auditable example of the same principle.
